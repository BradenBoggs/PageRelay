# Guests and collaboration with other organizations

Status: **Draft for owner review. Guest access is expansion; shared cross-organization channels are strategic scope. Neither is established as implemented.** The approved foundation remains one Organization per user. See [review scope](README.md).

This document owns restricted external participation, sponsorship, expiration, external-audience disclosure, and future shared-channel boundaries. It does not silently authorize a multi-organization account migration.

## Guests within one Organization

A guest is an explicitly restricted membership sponsored by a full member and invited by an authorized owner/administrator. An invitation identifies the Organization, sponsor, named allowed channel(s), available retained history, and access expiry. Guests do not join General, discover public channels broadly, or inherit full-member permissions.

Clearly label guests in member lists and conversation headers so participants understand when information is shared externally. A guest can read/post only in assigned channels and their authorized resources, subject to posting policy. Directory/search suggestions reveal only necessary shared-channel people and content. Invitations, billing, organization administration, primary page linking, broad mentions, exports, and app installation are unavailable by default.

Guest DMs are excluded initially. Adding them requires explicit allowed-contact rules and lifecycle behavior; sharing one channel does not automatically create a private communication relationship with everyone in the company.

## History and page context

Before adding a guest to an existing channel, show that its retained messages, files, threads, notes, and linked-page metadata may become visible. Prefer a separate external-facing channel when historical disclosure is inappropriate. An invitation must not silently copy a private internal chat into an external space.

Source URLs/titles can be sensitive even if the guest cannot log into the external service. Linking a page to a guest-accessible channel requires an explicit audience warning and appropriate manager approval. SideWire must not claim that external-service permissions protect metadata already shared in SideWire. Guests cannot discover hidden contexts through lookup failures or Apps counts.

## Expiration and account constraints

Sponsors/managers can review, extend, or revoke access. Show approaching expiry through the approved notification policy. Expiry revokes sessions, channel/resource access, calls, searches, and pending delivery, while preserving historical authorship for remaining authorized people. Reactivation requires explicit review of scope and expiry rather than restoring every past privilege.

A guest invitation addressed to someone already belonging to another SideWire Organization must not silently transfer their membership, create a parallel authority, or activate organization switching. Until an approved multi-organization or external-identity model exists, explain that the invitation cannot be completed under the current account model.

Guest billing is an open commercial decision in Billing. Do not copy Slack guest allowances or declare guests free, nonbillable, or discounted without approval.

## Future shared channels between companies

A shared external channel is different from placing guests inside one tenant. It requires explicit approval by both organizations, visible participating-company identities, defined administration rights, invitation/history boundaries, and compatible retention/export/deletion policies. Ordinary page linking must never create such a bridge.

Before implementation, define message ownership, which side may invite/remove people or apps, what history newcomers see, and what remains after disconnection. Disconnecting cannot promise to erase content the other organization already received. Cross-organization search, file delivery, AI, workflows, and identity switching require a separate security/model review; existing tenant isolation cannot be relaxed ad hoc.

## Acceptance behavior and decisions

A guest sees only approved channels/resources, understands the history shared, and loses future access at expiry. Internal metadata stays private through directory, source links, previews, and errors. Existing members of another Organization are not silently moved or granted a second membership. No external channel exists without both companies' explicit agreement and a defined data policy.

Owner decisions: guest scope, sponsor/inviter roles, expiry default, billing, guest DMs, and whether cross-company collaboration justifies changing the one-organization account model. Guests are not a public customer portal or anonymous chat service.

Reference coverage: [Slack guest roles](https://slack.com/help/articles/202518103-Understand-guest-roles-in-Slack) and [Slack external collaboration feature catalog](https://slack.com/features). SideWire rules are proposals, not copies of Slack entitlements.
